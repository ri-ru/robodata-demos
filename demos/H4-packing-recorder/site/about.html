Packing Demo Recorder.

An original interactive experiment about demonstration data and camera/action timing. The robot is the Apache-2.0 Franka Panda from Google DeepMind’s MuJoCo Menagerie. The page’s design, task, labels, box and parcels are original.

A real little world

The official @mujoco/mujoco 4.0.1 single-thread WebAssembly build integrates physics at 500 Hz. The arm uses the Menagerie joint position actuators with gravity/coriolis compensation and damped least-squares inverse kinematics. Two physical fingers pinch 55 g parcels with contact friction. Objects fall under gravity, collide, and must be released into the open box. Nothing is attached or teleported to the gripper.

Your demonstration

Keyboard control runs at 50 Hz. A recording stores the initial complete MuJoCo integration state, joint angles and velocities, controller targets, gripper commands, keyboard velocities, camera observations and timestamps. Replay restores the initial state and applies the recorded actuator controls through fresh physics steps. JSON stays in your browser until you download it.

The timing experiment

A camera observation contains the tool position, gripper aperture, and the three parcel positions. These are idealized object-tracking features from the scene, rather than pixels or a learned vision model. Moving the camera lag slider shifts the recorded camera timestamps later by 0–200 ms. Actions are then paired with the camera frame whose shifted timestamp is nearest to the action time. This deliberately tests dataset synchronization error.

The small policy performs a nearest-neighbour lookup on those feature/action pairs, using a local forward search window to preserve demonstration order. It selects a short future controller target, advances the real physics, and measures the resulting tool path against the recorded path. Runtime observations remain current: this experiment isolates wrong timestamps in the training data, rather than adding a second live sensor delay. A delay can teach the arm to close or move too soon. Some demonstrations tolerate lag better than others; the page reports measured results.
